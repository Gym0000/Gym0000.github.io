About me

I am a Ph.D. student in Cyberspace Security at the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC), advised by Prof. Honggang Hu. I received my B.Eng. in Information Security from USTC in 2023.

My research focuses on lattice algorithms and their applications to cryptanalysis, with a current emphasis on the Shortest Vector Problem (SVP), discrete Gaussian sampling, and Learning With Errors (LWE). I study how sampling, sieving, and decoding techniques can lead to faster algorithms with rigorous time and space bounds.

I also work on lattice-based methods in computational number theory, including integer factorization, RSA cryptanalysis, and small-root problems via Coppersmith's method. My broader interests lie in theoretical computer science, particularly subset sum and related problems.

  1. EUROCRYPT 2026

    Better Bounds for Finding Fixed-Degree Isogenies via Coppersmith’s Method

    Marius A. Aardal, Diego F. Aranha, Yansong Feng, Yiming Gao, and Yanbin Pan.

    DOIePrintCodeAuthors are listed alphabetically.
    Abstract

    The hardness of finding isogenies of degree ddd between supersingular elliptic curves is a fundamental assumption in isogeny-based cryptography. Let E1E_1E1​ and E2E_2E2​ be supersingular elliptic curves defined over Fp2\mathbb{F}_{p^2}Fp2​, and let ddd be a smooth integer. At CRYPTO 2024, Benčina et al. proposed an algorithm with time complexity O~(max⁡{p1/2,d/p5/8})\widetilde{O}(\max\{p^{1/2}, d/p^{5/8}\})O(max{p1/2,d/p5/8}) in the classical setting and O~(max⁡{p1/4,d1/2/p1/4})\widetilde{O}(\max\{p^{1/4}, d^{1/2}/p^{1/4}\})O(max{p1/4,d1/2/p1/4}) in the quantum setting.

    In this work, we first observe that their analysis omits a sub-exponential factor exp⁡(O(log⁡3/4p))\exp(O(\log^{3/4} p))exp(O(log3/4p)). We then improve their result to O~(max⁡{p1/2,exp⁡(O(log⁡4/5p))⋅d/p2/3})\widetilde{O}(\max\{p^{1/2}, \exp(O(\log^{4/5} p)) \cdot d/p^{2/3}\})O(max{p1/2,exp(O(log4/5p))⋅d/p2/3}) classically and O~(max⁡{p1/4,exp⁡(O(log⁡4/5p))⋅d1/2/p1/3})\widetilde{O}(\max\{p^{1/4}, \exp(O(\log^{4/5} p)) \cdot d^{1/2}/p^{1/3}\})O(max{p1/4,exp(O(log4/5p))⋅d1/2/p1/3}) quantumly. Our approach relies on small-root bounds for Coppersmith’s method applied to a four-variable integer equation. To this end, we adapt the explicit asymptotic formulas for small-root bounds introduced by Feng et al. (CRYPTO 2025) in the modular setting to the integer setting. As an additional application, we strengthen the attack of Benčina et al. on the SIDH signature scheme by Basso et al. (ACNS 2024). We expect that these refined techniques for Coppersmith’s method will be valuable for further post-quantum cryptanalysis.

    Abstract

    3.   3. ASIACRYPT 2024

    Attacking ECDSA with Nonce Leakage by Lattice Sieving: Bridging the Gap with Fourier Analysis-Based Attacks

    Yiming Gao†, Jinghui Wang†, Honggang Hu, and Binang He.

    DOIePrint† Equal contribution.
    Abstract

    The Hidden Number Problem (HNP) has found extensive applications in side-channel attacks against cryptographic schemes, such as ECDSA and Diffie-Hellman. There are two primary algorithmic approaches to solving the HNP: lattice-based attacks and Fourier analysis-based attacks. Lattice-based attacks exhibit better efficiency and require fewer samples when sufficiently long substrings of the nonces are known. However, they face significant challenges when only a small fraction of the nonce is leaked, such as 1-bit leakage, and their performance degrades in the presence of errors.

    In this paper, we address an open question by introducing an algorithmic tradeoff that significantly bridges the gap between these two approaches. By introducing a parameter xxx to modify Albrecht and Heninger's lattice, the lattice dimension is reduced by approximately (log⁡2x)/l(\log_2{x})/ l(log2​x)/l, where lll represents the number of leaked bits. We present a series of new methods, including the interval reduction algorithm, several predicates, and the pre-screening technique. Furthermore, we extend our algorithms to solve the HNP with erroneous input. Our attack outperforms existing state-of-the-art lattice-based attacks against ECDSA. We break several records including 1-bit and less than 1-bit leakage on a 160-bit curve, while the best previous lattice-based attack for 1-bit leakage was conducted only on a 112-bit curve.

    Yiming Gao, Xuyuan Han, and Honggang Hu.

    PDFarXivePrint
    Abstract

    We give classical randomized algorithms in a word-RAM model that break the 2n/22^{n/2}2n/2 barrier for both discrete Gaussian sampling (DGS) and the Shortest Vector Problem (SVP). For every fixed α>1\alpha>1α>1, shifted DGS on arbitrary lattices at width σ≥αη1/2(L)\sigma\ge\alpha\eta_{1/2}(L)σ≥αη1/2​(L) takes

    (3/2)n/2+o(n) timeand(4/3)n/2+o(n) space.(3/2)^{n/2+o(n)}\text{ time} \qquad\text{and}\qquad (4/3)^{n/2+o(n)}\text{ space}.(3/2)n/2+o(n) timeand(4/3)n/2+o(n) space.

At widths at least 3η1/2(L)/23\eta_{1/2}(L)/23η1/2​(L)/2, the same total cost suffices for an entire batch of ⌊(4/3)n/2⌋\lfloor(4/3)^{n/2}\rfloor⌊(4/3)n/2⌋ samples jointly close to independent target Gaussians.

    20.4130n+o(n) timeand20.3138n+o(n) space.2^{0.4130n+o(n)}\text{ time} \qquad\text{and}\qquad 2^{0.3138n+o(n)}\text{ space}.20.4130n+o(n) timeand20.3138n+o(n) space.

    As a corollary, we obtain centered DGS at every width with the same exponents, answering a question of Stephens-Davidowitz (SODA 2016).

    We introduce Gaussian difference dynamics (GDD), a framework for tracking expected counts and pairwise clustering of vectors through repeated differencing. A closed recursion for the first two counting moments controls these quantities relative to a Gaussian reference, without requiring independence within intermediate lists. Accurate moment bounds let us extract a Gaussian sample by weighted selection, while the weaker bounds needed to establish a shortest vector representation allow us to retain more differences. We perform these transitions on thin spherical shells, where the Gaussian rejection weights can be renormalized at subexponential cost. For batch sampling, we combine this geometry with the double rejection technique of Doulgerakis and Laarhoven to control the joint distribution of outputs while reusing vectors.

    ℓ∞\ell_{\infty}ℓ∞​ norm SVP in O~(3n)\widetilde{O}(3^n)O(3n)

    Yiming Gao.

    PDFarXiv
    Abstract

    We give a classical randomized algorithm for the Shortest Vector Problem in the infinity norm in O~(3n)\OO(3^n)O(3n) time and space, improving the previous bound of 22.443n+o(n)2^{2.443n+o(n)}22.443n+o(n).

    We adapt Gaussian difference dynamics to the infinity norm by repeatedly taking differences of vectors from two lists, producing lists in successively smaller cubes. The key is to restrict the midpoint of each pair to a fixed inner cube, so that every allowed difference has the same region of possible midpoints.     Abstract

    Aggarwal, Dadush, Regev, and Stephens-Davidowitz (ADRS) gave a 2n+o(n)2^{n+o(n)}2n+o(n) time algorithm for the Shortest Vector Problem (SVP) based on discrete Gaussian sampling (DGS), together with an honest sampler producing 2n/22^{n/2}2n/2 samples above the smoothing parameter in 2n/2+o(n)2^{n/2+o(n)}2n/2+o(n) time and space. We extend the product code analysis so that this guarantee is compatible with the claimed time and space bounds. This lets us target a rarer midpoint shell.     Provable Subexponential Algorithms for NIST Third-Round Lattice Families

    Yiming Gao, Xuyuan Han, and Honggang Hu.

    ePrint
    Abstract

    We give provable classical subexponential algorithms for secret recovery in growing parameter families associated with NIST third-round lattice candidates. For the Kyber/ML-KEM, FrodoKEM, SABER, NTRU LPRime, and Dilithium/ML-DSA families studied here, polynomial moduli and polylogarithmic coefficient scales yield recovery of the short secret component in expected time and space 2(1/2+o(1))n/ln⁡ln⁡n2^{(1/2+o(1))n/\ln\ln n}2(1/2+o(1))n/lnlnn.

    For noisy or rounded linear relations, we exploit an exact gap in the squared Euclidean norm of a comparison vector defined by each coordinate guess. One Gaussian list suffices to identify every secret coordinate by binary search, without enumerating the others. We establish the required sampling guarantees through new geometric bounds for structured public operators over prime and power of two moduli. The construction builds on the Wagner-style Gaussian sampling framework of Ducas, Engelberts, and Loyer (CRYPTO 2025) and the low-error decision-LWE algorithm of Han, Gao, and Hu (2026).

    For quotient relations of NTRU type, we develop an affine slice search: fixing coordinates restricts candidate pairs to slices of the public lattice, and their estimated Gaussian masses guide the choice of each next coordinate. In the stated modulus window, Falcon's key generation quality condition supplies the required mass bound. The algorithm then recovers an equivalent signing key with high probability in time and space 2O(n/ln⁡ln⁡n)2^{O(n/\ln\ln n)}2O(n/lnlnn). The same search recovers the short key core for cyclic NTRU-HPS/HRSS.

    Together, these results give subexponential algorithms for problem families associated with all seven NIST third-round lattice candidates. Despite the subexponential complexity, our results do not establish a reduction in the concrete security of the currently specified parameter sets.
